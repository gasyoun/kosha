Apte 1890

daivaApte p. 581 ↗
daiva a. (vī f.) [devādāgataḥ aṇ] Relating to gods, caused by or coming from gods, divine, celestial; saṃskṛtaṃ nāma daivī vāganvākhyātā maharṣibhiḥ  Kāv. 1. 33 ; daivīnāṃ mānuṣīṇāṃ ca pratihartā tvamāpadāṃ  R. 1. 60 ;  Y. 2. 235 ;  Bg. 4. 25 , 9. 13 , 16. 3 ;  Ms. 3. 75. 
— vaḥ (i. e. vivāhaḥ) One of the eight forms of marriage, that in which the daughter is given away at a sacrifice to the officiating priest; yajñasya ṛtvije daivaḥ  Y. 1. 59 ; (for the eight forms of marriage see udvāha or  Ms. 3. 21 ).
— vaṃ
1 Fate, destiny, luck, fortune; daivamavidvāṃsaḥ pramāṇayaṃti  Mu. 3 ; vinā puruṣakāreṇa daivamatra na sidhyati ‘God helps those who help
(pb) themselves’; daivaṃ nihatya kuru pauruṣama tmaśaktyā  Pt. 1. 361.  (daivāt by chance, luckily, accidentally).
2 A god, deity.
3 A religious rite or offering, an oblation to gods.
4 A kind of Śrāddha ceremony.
5 Parts of the hands sacred to the gods, i. e. the tips of the fingers; cf.  Ms. 2. 59. 
— vī A woman married according to the form of marriage called daiva q. v. above.
Comp.

— ahorātraḥ a day of the gods. i. e. the human year.
— upahata a. illfated, unfortunate;  Mu. 6. 8. 
— karnan n. offering oblations to gods.

1 fated.
2 natural.
— kovid, -ciṃtakaḥ, -jñaḥ an astrologer, a fortune-teller;  Y. 1. 313 ;  Kām. 9. 25.

— taṃtra a. dependent on fate.
