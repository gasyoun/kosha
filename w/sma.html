Monier-Williams

smaMW p. 1271 ↗
sma, smā, (or ṣma, ṣmā), ind. a particle perhaps originally equivalent to ‘ever’, ‘always’;
and later to ‘indeed’, ‘certainly’, ‘verily’, ‘surely’ (it is often used pleonastically, and in earlier language generally follows a similar particle [esp. ha, na], or relative, or prep. or verb, while in later language it frequently follows iti, na and mā [cf. 1. mā]; it is also joined with a pres. tense or pres. participle to give them a past sense [e.g. praviśanti sma, ‘they entered’]; this use of sma is also found in the Brāhmaṇas and is extended to veda and āha cf.  Vām. v, 2, 46 ),  RV. ; &c. &c.

Apte 1890

smaApte p. 1155 ↗
sma ind.
1 A particle added to the present tense of verbs (or to present participles) and giving them the sense of the past tense; bhāsurako nāma siṃhaḥ prativasati sma  Pt. 1 ; krīṇaṃti sma prāṇamūlyairyaśāṃsi  Śi. 18. 15. 
2 A pleonastic particle (generally added to the prohibitive particle mā q. v.); bharturviprakṛtāpi roṣaṇatayā māsma pratīpaṃ gamaḥ  Ś. 4. 17 ; māsma sīmaṃtinī kācijjatayetputramīdṛśaṃ  H. 2. 7.
