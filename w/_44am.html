dham or dhmā cl. 1. P. dhamati (Ā. ˚te,  Up. ;  MBh. ; p. dhmāntas = dhamantas,  BhP. x, 12, 7 ; perf. dadhmau, 3. pl. Ā. ˚mire,  MBh. ; aor. adhmāsīt,  Kāv. ; Prec. dhmāyāt or dhmeyāt Gr.; fut. dhamiṣyati,  MBh. ; dhmāsyati, dhmātā Gr.; ind.p. -dhmāya,  Br. )
to blow (either intrans. as wind [applied also to the bubbling Soma,  RV. ix, 73 ] or trans. as, to blow a conch-shell or any wind instrument),  RV.  &c. &c.;
to blow into (loc.),  MBh. l, 813 ;
to breathe out, exhale,  RV. ii, 34, 1 ;  MBh. xiv, 1732 ;
to kindle a fire by blowing,  RV. ii, 24, 7 ;  MBh. ii, 2483 ;
to melt or manufacture (metal) by blowing,  RV.  &c. &c.;
to blow or cast away,  MBh. v, 7209  :
Pass. dhamyate, ep. also ˚ti, dhmāyate, ˚ti ( ŚBr. ;  MBh. ) to be blown &c.:
Caus. dhmāpayati,  MBh.  (aor. adidhmapat Gr.; Pass. dhmāpyate,  MBh. )
to cause to blow or melt;
to consume by fire, reduce to cinder,  MBh. ;  Suśr.  :
Desid. didhmāsati Gr.:
Intens. dedhmīyate,  Pāṇ. vii, 4, 31 ;
dādhmāyate, p. ˚yamāna being violently blown (conch-shell),  BhP. i, 11, 2. 
[cf. Slav. dumo ‘smoke’]
