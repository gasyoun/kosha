1. vāyu m. (fr. √2. vā) wind, air (as one of the 5 elements; in  MBh.  7 winds are reckoned),  RV.  &c. &c.
vāyuscan ↗
the god of the wind (often associated with Indra in the Ṛg-veda, as Vāta [q.v.] with Parjanya, but although of equal rank with Indra, not occupying so prominent a position; in the Puruṣasūkta he is said to have sprung form the breath of Puruṣa, and elsewhere is described as the son-in-law of Tvaṣṭṛ; be is said to move in a shining car drawn by a pair of red or purple horses or by several teams consisting of ninety-nine or a hundred or even a thousand horses [cf. ni-yut]; he is often made to occupy the same chariot with Indra, and in conjunction with him honoured with the first draught of the Soma libation; he is rarely connected with the Maruts, although in i, 134, 4 , he is said to have begotten them from the rivers of heaven; he is regent of the Nakṣatra Svāti and north-west quarter See loka-pāla),  ib. 
vāyuscan ↗
breathing, breath,  VPrāt. ;  ĪśUp. 
vāyuscan ↗
the wind of the body, a vital air (of which 5 are reckoned, viz. prāRa, apāna, samāna, udāna, and vyāna; or nāga, kūrma, kṛkara, devadatta, and dhanaṃ-jaya),  Hariv. ;  Sāṃkhyak. ;  Vedāntas. 
vāyuscan ↗
(in medicine) the windy humour or any morbid affection of it,  Suśr. 
vāyuscan ↗
the wind as a kind of demon producing madness,  Kād. ;  Vcar.  (cf. -grasta)
vāyuscan ↗
(in astron.) N. of the fourth Muhūrta

vāyuscan ↗
of a Marut,  R. 
vāyuscan ↗
pl. the Maruts,  Kathās. ;  MārkP. 
vāyuscan ↗
2. vāyu mfn. (fr. √vai) tired, languid,  RV. vii, 91, 1. 
vāyuscan ↗
3. vāyu mfn. (fr. √vī) desirous, covetous, greedy (for food, applied to calves),  TS.

desirable, desired by the appetite,  RV.
