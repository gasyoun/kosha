What a red cell does and does not mean. It means the form does not occur in the 5.69M-token DCS corpus. It is not a claim that the form is wrong or that the generator is broken: most of Sanskrit's paradigm space is simply unattested in any finite corpus — 47.17% of the cells shown here are attested, and 57 of the head lemmas have no attested cell at all. Read it as corpus coverage, not as a defect list.

Two scope facts that bound every reading, carried from the audit. (1) Verbs. The generator holds 680 verbal lemmas against 222,736 nominal, so finite verbs are territory it never claimed; a verbal gap is out of scope, not a bug — gap rows carry their DCS upos. (2) Sandhi. A paradigm generator emits unsandhied forms while DCS records the sandhied surface, so gap rows attested only as a surface (via: surface) are shown dimmed — for those the mismatch is an artefact of the attested side. Of the full 196,378-key gap set, 51.06% are surface-only and the defensible give-back subset is 5,656 rows, method in the candidates report.

Homographic cells share one evidence count — read ≈ carefully. The join key is form_key(), the length-preserving floor tier, which folds the final visarga and homorganic nasals. So rāmaḥ (nom. sg.) and rāma (voc. sg.) reduce to the same key and are credited with the same occurrence count — the corpus evidence says "this key occurs 3,325 times", not "3,325 of them are vocatives". Any cell matched below a byte-identical SLP1 key carries ≈; where two cells of one paradigm show an identical count, that is this fold, not a claim that both functions are equally attested. Case-level disambiguation needs the DCS morphological tags, which this page deliberately does not infer.

R-C4: DCS conflates aorist and perfect under Tense=Past; cells whose evidence carries a Past-tense token are flagged ▵ rather than excluded.
